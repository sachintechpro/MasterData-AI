01 / THE PROBLEM

One customer. Three versions of the truth.

Let's talk about Master Data Management (MDM).

If you love MDM, you probably appreciate clean data, golden records, identity resolution, governance, and the promise of a consistent customer view across an enterprise.

If you hate MDM, you've probably experienced the other side: duplicate records, endless reconciliation, complicated integrations, conflicting ownership rules, and systems that still cannot agree on something as simple as a customer's address.

Both sides have a point. And that's exactly why I built Column & Key.

They may all represent the same person. But should an enterprise simply assume that? What happens when their addresses differ? When two customers share a phone number? When a matching algorithm confidently links the wrong individuals?

This is where MDM becomes interesting: not because it creates another database, but because it introduces identity, trust, rules, traceability, and accountability across disconnected systems.

02 / CHOOSE YOUR ROUTE

MDM lover or MDM hater?

No certification required. You can explore the same working application from two very different perspectives.

THE OPTIMIST'S PATH / BUILD THE GOLDEN RECORD

Make the connections. Keep the evidence.

Start in Customer 360 and find Michael Johnson. Initially, the policy and billing identities aren't approved, so their transactions don't appear in his mastered view.

Visit Match review. Inspect the candidate identities, compare the names and other evidence, and approve a link when it looks justified. Return to Customer 360 to see new policies, claims, or invoices from the linked records.

The original source IDs haven't disappeared. Their provenance remains available. A golden record should establish trust, not erase the evidence.

THE SKEPTIC'S PATH / TRY TO BREAK IT

Question every match. Find the cracks.

Look at Helena Ortiz. Source records match on several identity attributes but disagree on the address. Does that justify linking them? Which system gets to decide which address wins?

Review the candidate, then approve or reject the suggestion. Examine the conflict and the evidence preserved in the record. Ask who made the decision and whether an algorithm should ever be allowed to merge identities automatically.

A high matching score is not proof of identity. The real work is deciding what the evidence means—and what the enterprise is allowed to do with it.

03 / NOW ADD ONE INTERFACE

What if people could just ask?

What if business users didn't need to know which system held which piece of information? What if they could ask a question through one interface?

ASK 01 Are there any open claims? ↗ASK 02 Does this customer have unpaid invoices? ↗ASK 03 Where did this information come from? ↗

Column & Key uses approved identity links to retrieve facts from its CSV sources and show the supporting records and technical trace. It uses a transparent rule-based question handler by default, with optional LLM integration.

MDM establishes trusted identity. Enterprise applications retain transactional facts. A conversational interface makes them accessible.

A chatbot alone doesn't manufacture trustworthy enterprise data. If the underlying identities are wrong, a polished answer can be wrong about the wrong person.

04 / UNDER THE HOOD

Deliberately simple. Technically inspectable.

No commercial MDM license. No heavyweight data platform. No live Salesforce or Guidewire integration. The purpose is to expose the mechanics that enterprise architecture diagrams often hide.

01 / DATAEight fictional CSV files

Customer, policy, claim, billing, identity-link, and review-decision records.

02 / APIPython + REST

Customer joins, match decisions, data access, and explainable evidence.

03 / GOVERNANCEHuman-reviewed matching

Deterministic scores, approvals, rejection, source provenance, and conflicts.

04 / QUALITYExecutable checks

API and business-flow tests, plus on-demand CSV integrity checks.

The simple architecture is intentional. This is a working proof of concept, not a replacement for a commercial MDM product or a claim of production-grade enterprise integration.

05 / THE TRUST TEST

Trust the record. Not the dashboard.

Try a simple experiment. Start with an unlinked customer identity and ask about their policies or billing information. Then review and approve an appropriate identity match and ask the same question again.

Compare the answers. Inspect the source rows. Notice how identity resolution determines what the assistant can reliably tell you.

YOUR CHALLENGEDoes your answer change when you change what the system is allowed to know?Run the experiment ↗

When an incorrect identity link causes an incorrect answer, that's not only an AI problem. It's a data-governance problem that AI makes harder to ignore.

06 / WHY I BUILT IT

Make invisible decisions visible.

My interest sits at the intersection of enterprise systems, business workflows, integration, quality engineering, and practical AI applications.

In enterprise environments, customer information moves across CRM, insurance policy systems, billing, service platforms, and integration layers. The hard part is rarely displaying a customer name. It's knowing how records relate, who owns a field, what happens when systems disagree, and whether downstream applications can trust the result.

Column & Key is a small experiment in making those questions visible, testable, and approachable—without pretending that the underlying decisions are simple.
